# NES / Famicom

*1983–1995* · Stock, the front-loader only has composite and RF: a soft, smeared picture. Video mods make the biggest difference.

| Model | Note |
|---|---|
| **NES-001** | Front-loader (NTSC) |
| **NES-101** | Top-loader, RF only |
| **HVC-001 / HVC-101** | Famicom / AV Famicom (Japan) |

### NESRGB

Board that sits under the PPU and adds RGB with selectable palettes (6 on the 2022 version), plus dejitter for better scaler compatibility. Often paired with a multi-out so you can use SNES cables.

<div class="pn-rate" markdown>
<span class="pn-k">Community</span> <span class="pn-stars pn-r5">★★★★★</span> **Must-have** · For years the reference NES RGB mod: mature, well documented, palettes and dejitter. Pricier, and the PPU must come out.

<span class="pn-k">Install</span> <span class="pn-bar pn-d4">■■■■</span> **Expert** · Desoldering large chips or lifting chip pins, board rework. Good desoldering tools needed.
</div>

### Hi-Def NES

Digital HDMI output (up to 1080p) with palettes, scanlines and an on-screen menu. The sharpest option.

<div class="pn-rate" markdown>
<span class="pn-k">Community</span> <span class="pn-stars pn-r4">★★★★☆</span> **Recommended** · The sharpest NES picture over HDMI, and expansion audio without an extra mod. Purists prefer analog RGB with a good scaler.

<span class="pn-k">Install</span> <span class="pn-bar pn-d4">■■■■</span> **Expert** · Desoldering large chips or lifting chip pins, board rework. Good desoldering tools needed.
</div>

### AV mod (top-loader, Famicom)

Adds the composite (and sometimes S-Video) output that the NES-101 and the original Famicom are missing.

<div class="pn-rate" markdown>
<span class="pn-k">Community</span> <span class="pn-stars pn-r4">★★★★☆</span> **Recommended** · Cheap and a big step up from RF on the top-loader and Famicom. RGB mods go further.

<span class="pn-k">Install</span> <span class="pn-bar pn-d2">■■□□</span> **Intermediate** · Basic soldering: a few wires or through-hole parts.
</div>

## Audio

### Expansion audio

Wires the expansion port so Famicom expansion sound chips (VRC6, etc.) play through the NES, with a flash cart or adapter.

<div class="pn-rate" markdown>
<span class="pn-k">Community</span> <span class="pn-stars pn-r3">★★★☆☆</span> **Good, with caveats** · Simple and worth it if you play Famicom games with sound chips on a flash cart. Otherwise it changes nothing.

<span class="pn-k">Install</span> <span class="pn-bar pn-d2">■■□□</span> **Intermediate** · Basic soldering: a few wires or through-hole parts.
</div>

### 10NES disable (CIC)

Cuts one pin on the lockout chip. Ends the boot-blinking caused by the chip and lets other-region carts run.

<div class="pn-rate" markdown>
<span class="pn-k">Community</span> <span class="pn-stars pn-r5">★★★★★</span> **Must-have** · Free, quick and recommended on every front-loader. Pairs well with a refurbished connector.

<span class="pn-k">Install</span> <span class="pn-bar pn-d2">■■□□</span> **Intermediate** · Basic soldering: a few wires or through-hole parts.
</div>

## Maintenance & reliability

### 72-pin connector

Refurbishing or replacing the cartridge connector: the main cure for the blinking grey screen.

<div class="pn-rate" markdown>
<span class="pn-k">Community</span> <span class="pn-stars pn-r5">★★★★★</span> **Must-have** · The real cure for blinking. Most prefer refurbishing the original: cheap new connectors are often too tight and wear carts.

<span class="pn-k">Install</span> <span class="pn-bar pn-d1">■□□□</span> **Easy** · Screwdriver (often a game bit), drop-in parts, no soldering.
</div>
