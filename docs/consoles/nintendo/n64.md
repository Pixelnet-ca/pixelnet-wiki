# Nintendo 64

*1996–2002* · North American N64s have no RGB stock and a blurry picture. Digital HDMI or RGB mods transform it.

| Model | Note |
|---|---|
| **NUS-CPU-01 to -03** | Early boards: easier RGB |
| **NUS-CPU-04 and later** | Need a digital RGB board |

**Legend:** :material-wrench: **PixelNet installs it** · :material-book-open-variant: **Guide** on this wiki · :material-power-plug: **No install** (cart, adapter or software) · :material-cart: **Buy** from the official source

## Video

### Retro G.E.M.

Internal HDMI kit that taps the console's digital video. Basic version (720p, great with an external scaler) or Shiny version (full built-in scaling). With a PixelFX Morph 4K, adds Game-ID and per-game settings.

<span class="pn-k">Community</span> <span class="pn-stars pn-r5">★★★★★</span> **Must-have** · Considered the best current N64 HDMI solution, with more settings than older boards. Pricey; check your board revision is supported.

### N64RGB Kit

RGB amplifier with built-in sync stripper for the early boards (NUS-CPU-01 to -03). Works with a standard SNES csync cable.

<div class="pn-rate" markdown>

## Region & frequency

### Cartridge slot trim

Removes the slot tabs so Japanese carts fit a North American console (same NTSC lockout).

<div class="pn-rate" markdown>
<span class="pn-k">Community</span> <span class="pn-stars pn-r4">★★★★☆</span> **Recommended** · Free and easy for Japanese carts. Permanent.

<span class="pn-k">Install</span> <span class="pn-bar pn-d1">■□□□</span> **Easy** · Screwdriver (often a game bit), drop-in parts, no soldering.
</div>
